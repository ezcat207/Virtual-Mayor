# Candidate business paths to ~$1M ARR

Honesty notice: every price, conversion rate and customer count below is a SPECULATIVE assumption unless tied to a cited competitor price (see competitors.md). Nothing here is validated demand. "Virtual Mayor" as a game/education product on its own has weak monetization (see Path 5); the money is in using the same asset (deep, structured per-city knowledge + simulation + AI-governance map) as a work tool for people who earn from city decisions.

Core asset reused across paths: a per-city "dossier engine" (budget, zoning, politics, officials, digital/AI governance, procurement), plus a mayor-role simulator and AI interviewer as the interface.

Math convention: customers needed = $1,000,000 / annual price per customer. Add 20-30% for churn in practice.

---
## Path 1: City intelligence for GovTech / civic-AI vendor sales teams (B2B)
- Who pays: AE/SDRs and sales-enablement leads at GovTech and AI vendors selling to cities (Polimorphic, Granicus-type, CAD/permitting, 311 vendors, and long tail of ~hundreds of small vendors). They make money by closing city contracts (contract sizes $5k-$35k+ per the 2025 pricing guide).
- Product: "Know the mayor before the call": per-city brief (priorities, budget, council dynamics, tech stack, AI policy, upcoming RFPs), plus a roleplay simulator where the rep practices pitching a simulated mayor/CIO grounded in that city's data.
- Price (assumption): $3,000-$6,000 per seat-team per year; example $4,800/yr team of 3.
- Customers for $1M: ~210 teams at $4,800 (or 50 enterprise accounts at $20k).
- Distribution: LinkedIn outbound to GovTech sales leaders, Smart Cities Dive / Cities Today sponsorships, GovTech conferences, partnerships with procurement-data tools.
- How AI makes them money: faster account research, tailored pitches, higher win rate; AI drafts RFP responses (see Civio, Cleatus tools).
- Risks: data freshness and accuracy per city (hard, ongoing labor); incumbents (Granicus, procurement-intel tools) can add it; small total market of GovTech vendors; sales cycles 2-4 months.
- 30-day experiment: build 10 city briefs by hand+AI for one vertical (e.g. 311 chatbots for mid-size US cities). Cold-message 60 sales leaders offering 3 free briefs for a 20-minute call. Success: 8+ calls, 3 would pay $300+ per brief, 1 paid pilot. Also ask for a $500 prepaid.

## Path 2: Site-selection and zoning dossiers for small developers and real-estate investors
- Who pays: small developers, infill/ADU investors, land flippers, expansion site selectors (franchise operators, self-storage, EV charging, data-center brokers). They make money from picking the right parcel/city.
- Product: city-level "rules of the game" dossier (zoning reform status, permit speed, politics of the council, fees, pipeline, AI/digital permitting maturity) plus simulation of "what would this mayor/council approve". Not parcel-level (Archistar, Giraffe, TestFit, Regrid already do that).
- Price: $99-$299/month (Archistar charges A$95-A$595/mo; Giraffe $1,000-$3,000/yr; TestFit Parking Solver $195/mo).
- Customers for $1M: at $199/mo ($2,388/yr) ~420 subscribers; at $2,000/yr one-off city reports, 500 reports.
- Distribution: BiggerPockets, r/realestateinvesting, YouTube creators, local REIA meetups, newsletters; SEO "[city] zoning/permit process" programmatic pages.
- How AI makes them money: faster due diligence, finding underpriced entitlement opportunities, drafting variance/rezoning arguments to officials.
- Risks: crowded with generic AI zoning tools; liability if the brief is wrong; investors want parcel data, not city culture; consumer-ish churn.
- 30-day experiment: pick 5 cities, produce a $149 "Entitlement Brief" per city, sell via 2 BiggerPockets posts + 30 DMs. Success: 10 sales ($1,490) or 25 email deposits.

## Path 3: AI toolkit and training for independent planning / policy consultants
- Who pays: solo and small-firm planning consultants, policy analysts, grant writers serving cities (hourly $75-$300+ per ZipRecruiter/consulting benchmarks, speculative for planners specifically). They make money by delivering comprehensive plans, grant applications and studies faster.
- Product: cohort course + template library ("Comprehensive plan in 2 weeks with AI": zoning code review, community-engagement synthesis, grant narrative, council-memo simulation using the mayor simulator for stakeholder rehearsal).
- Price: $1,500 cohort or $49/mo community.
- Customers for $1M: ~670 cohort students at $1,500, or ~1,700 members at $49/mo. Planner population is limited (APA membership is ~40k, unverified), so this needs ~2% penetration: ambitious.
- Distribution: LinkedIn, APA chapters, Planetizen, YouTube; webinars.
- How AI makes them money: lowers cost per deliverable, lets one person bid on more RFPs.
- Risks: planners are modestly paid and cost-sensitive; public-sector budgets slow; AI-skeptic culture; cheap APA courses anchor price ($25-$245).
- 30-day experiment: free 90-min live workshop to 50 consultants, then pre-sell a 4-week cohort at $499. Success: 5 paid seats.

## Path 4: Civic-AI advisory for city governments (services-led)
- Who pays: city managers/CIOs of small and mid-size cities who must write AI policy and buy tools (Kirkland smart-city master plan RFP and Stanwood AI permitting RFQ are examples).
- Product: "AI governance sprint": benchmark against peer cities' digital/AI governance (our dataset), draft policy, run a mayor/council simulation workshop. Productize later into subscription.
- Price: $10k-$25k per engagement (assumption; check against RFP awards).
- Customers for $1M: ~50-100 engagements/yr. For one founder this is capacity-bound (maybe 10-15/yr alone).
- Distribution: MRSC-type municipal associations, state leagues of cities, RFP portals (BidNet, SAM), referrals from GovTech vendors.
- How AI makes them money: it is the consultants (you) who make money; AI cuts research time 5-10x.
- Risks: slow procurement, political cycles, services do not scale, needs credibility/references.
- 30-day experiment: respond to 3 live RFPs/RFQs or offer 5 cities a free 1-page AI-governance benchmark; success is 2 discovery calls with a budget holder.

## Path 5: Consumer education: students (MPA/MUP), civics learners, city nerds
- Who pays: grad students and enthusiasts. They do not "make money with AI" directly, so this fits the founder thesis poorly; value is future career.
- Price: $10-$15/mo or $99/yr; university site licenses $2k-$10k (assumption).
- Customers for $1M: ~10,000 subs at $99/yr, or ~200 university licenses at $5k. Consumer distribution needs a hit game or viral content.
- Distribution: Steam/itch, TikTok/YouTube/Bilibili city content, university faculty outreach, iCivics-like free tier.
- How AI makes them money: indirect (career skills).
- Risks: lowest ARPU, highest CAC, game production cost, Democracy 4 sold ~58k in six months at ~$30, implying a hit takes years; Hello History-like pricing ($4-$10/mo) caps revenue.
- 30-day experiment: ship a free browser prototype for 3 cities, post on r/urbanplanning, r/CitiesSkylines, Bilibili. Success: 1,000 plays, 100 email signups, 5 faculty replies.

## Path 6: Creator tools: city-content creators (YouTube, Bilibili, newsletters)
- Who pays: creators who monetize city/urbanism/travel/finance content (ads, sponsorships). They make money from audience attention.
- Product: per-city research packs + AI "interview the mayor" script generator, B-roll prompts, fact-checked data cards.
- Price: $29/mo.
- Customers for $1M: ~2,900 subscribers: hard, creator niche is small and price-sensitive.
- Distribution: creator communities, YouTube/X, 知识星球/Bilibili in China.
- Risks: niche too small; creators already use generic ChatGPT; low willingness to pay.
- 30-day experiment: 10 creators get free packs in exchange for a testimonial and revenue-sharing talk; success: 3 would pay $29.

## Ranking (honest, subjective)
1. Path 1 (GovTech vendor sales intelligence): best ACV, identifiable buyers with existing budget and clear money-making link (closed deals), and the simulation is a natural sales-training hook. Weakness: data upkeep and small market. Needs only ~200 customers.
2. Path 4 (civic-AI advisory) as a cash-flow bridge and data-gathering engine for Path 1: fastest to first revenue, but does not scale alone. Run as the first 3-6 months.
3. Path 2 (developer/investor dossiers): large market, but crowded and data-heavy; viable if niche to a specific investor type (e.g. ADU investors, one state).
4. Path 3 (consultant training): plausible one-time revenue, lower ceiling per customer, small population.
5. Path 6, 6. Path 5: weakest fit to "help people make money with AI" and highest customer counts.

Recommended plan: Month 1 run Path 1 and Path 4 experiments together using the same 10 city briefs. Keep the mayor simulator as the demo/interface, not the product. Kill any path with fewer than 3 paying or prepaying customers after 30 days. Target: $1M ARR is a 2-3 year goal on Path 1 (about 200 teams at ~$5k) and is not a safe assumption.

## Key unknowns to resolve first
- Does a GovTech rep or developer pay for city knowledge when ChatGPT/Perplexity can answer "tell me about city X"? Differentiator must be verified, structured, current data plus a trained simulator.
- Data licensing and accuracy liability.
- Whether the founder wants a US, global or China-focused market; China-side (注册城乡规划师 exam prep, 知识星球 communities) is a separate go-to-market with different regulation and is not scored here.
